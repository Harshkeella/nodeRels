On this slide, we're looking at the sigmoid function, also known as the logistic function. This function takes any real number, z, and maps it to a value between 0 and 1, but not including 0 or 1. As z gets really big and positive, the value of e to the power of -z gets very, very small, which means that the sigmoid function, σ(z), gets really close to 1. Conversely, when z is a very large negative number, e to the power of -z becomes a really big number, pulling σ(z) towards 0. At z equals 0, the function hits exactly 0.5, which is a natural decision midpoint.

One of the really useful things about the sigmoid function is that it has a special property: its own derivative. This self-referential derivative is exactly why the gradient computation later on turns out so clean.
