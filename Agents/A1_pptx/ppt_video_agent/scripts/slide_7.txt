Now, let's talk about the full cost function. This function comes from a method called maximum likelihood estimation. Imagine each label in our data as a Bernoulli outcome, which means it can either be 0 or 1. The probability of each label is given by h of theta, which is our hypothesis function. The likelihood of the entire training set is the product of these probabilities for all the examples in our set.

However, directly maximizing this product can be tricky because multiplying many small probabilities together can result in a very small number, which might cause numerical issues. So, instead of maximizing the product, we maximize its logarithm. This is a common technique to avoid underflow problems. And to turn the problem into a minimization one, we flip the sign, which gives us the log loss, also known as binary cross-entropy.

The cost function J of theta is the average of these log losses over all the examples in our training set. Because this J of theta is a convex function, we can use gradient-based optimization methods to find the global minimum, which will give us the best parameters for our model.

So, in summary, the full cost function J of theta, which is the average of the log losses, is a way to optimize our model parameters. And because it's convex, we can be sure that the optimization methods will find the global minimum.
